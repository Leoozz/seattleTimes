Household Robot NEO Under Fire After Scalding 6-Year-Old in Omaha Kitchen Accident

by CORY SMITH | The National News Desk | November 12th 2025 at 2:30 PM

A tragic accident in a small town near Omaha, Nebraska has reignited debate over the safety of domestic robots.

According to early reports, a Robot NEO, a recently launched home assistant designed to help with household chores, accidentally spilled a pot of boiling water on a six-year-old child while attempting to grab an object in the family’s kitchen. The child sustained serious burns to the head and was immediately taken to a nearby hospital.

While medical staff have confirmed the child is receiving treatment, no official statement has been made about their current condition, leaving many concerned about the extent of the injuries.


The NEO robot was in this household as part of a limited pre-release testing program, in which the company sent 10 prototype units to 10 selected families before the product’s official public release planned for 2026. The public does not yet have access to the robot, as only preorders have been made available so far.

The NEO robot, promoted as one of the most advanced home-assistant technologies on the market, was developed to handle everyday domestic duties such as cooking assistance, folding clothes, and organizing spaces.

This accident, however, has raised new questions about the robot’s ability to safely operate in environments involving heat, liquids, and human presence, especially around young children.

Following the incident, the company behind NEO has paused all pre-orders and announced that an internal investigation is underway. As of now, no public statement has been posted on the company’s official social media channels.


Experts in robotics and AI ethics have already weighed in, noting that while automation can improve daily life, insufficient safety protocols and inadequate environmental awareness could lead to dangerous situations as these machines become more common in households.

The Omaha case serves as a wake-up call for the robotics industry, underscoring the urgent need for stricter safety standards, improved sensor technology, and real-world testing before releasing such devices to the public.

For many, it’s a reminder that innovation must move hand-in-hand with responsibility — especially when it enters the most sensitive environments of all: our homes.
